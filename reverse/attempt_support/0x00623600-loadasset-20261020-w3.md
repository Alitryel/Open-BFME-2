## What changed (each a measured step)

- **`char type[5]`** instead of `char type[8]`. The fourcc plus its terminator is five bytes. With eight, cl placed the find-key/profile-reference slot at esp+0x24 and the `this` spill and type buffer at +0x28. With five, it uses retail's order: `this` and type at +0x24, key, tStart reference and the final lock at +0x2c. This is the "natural storage type" lever. Moving the type declaration (top of the function, after `t[9]`, before `tEnd`) instead grew or reshuffled the frame.
- **The tInit column reads `t[8] - tStart`.** Retail at +0x8ef reads `[esp+0x78]` with 8 bytes pushed, which is base +0x70, the ninth element of `t` at +0x30. WorldBuilder 0x167B5C0 reads the same element (+0x68 with its `t` at +0x28). Behaviour is unchanged, because `t[8]` only ever holds the `t[0]` copy.
- **Phase gaps print when `t[i] < t[i+1]`.** The 0.8 body's `t[i+1] >= t[i]` reversed all nine jcc conditions (9 of its 15 regions). The 0.7 bank already had the `<` form.
- **`Rva000309B0` is modelled as a class with the pinned ctor and dtor** (`??0Rva000309B0@@QAE@I@Z` at 0x30980 and `??1Rva000309B0@@QAE@XZ` at 0x309B0), declared as in `Code/GameEngine/Source/Common/GameEngineInit.cpp`. The bytes are the same as the inline wrapper's, but it is the faithful form: retail 0x30980 returns `this`, and EH state 0 is entered after it.
- **Renamed** `AssetRegistry` to `AssetManagerImpl` (WorldBuilder's assert string `"AssetManagerImpl::LoadAsset"`) and the +0x08 member to `m_assetID` (WorldBuilder assert `"!(!asset.m_assetID)"` over `[esi+8]`). The element class keeps the Code-wide address name `Rva009EF0D0Element`. `AssetLoadEntry` in the earlier rows' symbol appears nowhere in `Code/`.

## Remaining mismatch

There is one scratch-register rotation, retail +0x44f through +0x5b0: the `queue == 7` reset of the phase byte, the case 4 body, and cases 5, 6, 0 and 1 of the phase chain. Retail takes edx for the reset temp; ours takes eax. Every later case then runs one step behind in the eax/ecx/edx cycle, until the call setup in case 1 (+0x5ad, `mov [edi+4],ecx` / `mov ecx,edi` swap). From case 2 onward both agree.

The eax forms are shorter (`and eax, imm32` has a short encoding), so ours is 2 bytes short at +0x669. Each 16-byte loop-alignment pad that retail emits (+0x669 with 7 bytes, +0x80c with 4, and the one before the jump table) then falls past cl's pad limit and disappears. That is the whole 16-byte deficit and all six remaining regions. Everything outside the rotation window matches, apart from relocations.

## Ruled out this run (the rotation stays eax)

Each was compiled on top of the current body. Unless noted, every one gave byte-identical output.

- **Phase field form:** mask forms of the queued bit and of the reset, both through `m_bfmeFlags` and through `m_bfmeFlagsShared`, and `= true`.
- **Accessors:** inline `SetPhase`/`GetPhase` accessors for all phase reads and writes, and an inline `SetLoading()`.
- **Parameter:** a reference parameter (`Rva009EF0D0Element &asset`). WorldBuilder's `asset.m_assetID` hints at one, and it gives the same bytes under the mangled name `...AAV...`.
- **`queue` type:** `int` gives the same bytes. `unsigned char` breaks the frame.
- **Scoped ctor declaration:** `int` or pointer parameter, `explicit`, a multichar `'asst'` literal, copy-initialisation, and `throw()` on the ctor. `throw()` on the dtor drops an EH store and is worse.
- **Scoped object and tag spellings:** the scoped object as a member of an inline wrapper class, a `const &` bound to a temporary scoped object, the tag held in a `const` local, and the tag built by an inline `MakeFourCC('a','s','s','t')`.
- **Queued bit through a reference:** a `volatile unsigned int &` to the flags word with `|= 0x2000000`.
- **Switch:** the switch on a named phase local (`unsigned` and `int`), braces on the reset, an explicit `case 3:`, and dropping `default:`.
- **Declaration order:** moving the `Rva000309B0`, `CriticalSectionLock` and element class declarations before or after `AssetManagerImpl`.
- **Final lock scope:** an explicit block around the final lock, and a block from the heap scope to the end. The latter moves the `this` spill to +0x18.
- **Profiling init:** loop bounds `<= 8`, an `unsigned` index, `t[k] = t[k - 1]`, the `!= 0` and `*g_bfmeProfileFile` tests, and the queued store moved before the profiling block (worse).
- **Unused or copied locals:** one to four unused locals at the top or before the switch, and one or two copied asset pointers at the top, at the queued store or at the reset.
- **Flags:** `/G5`, no `/G`, `/G6 /arch:SSE` and `/Ogityb2` give the same bytes. `/G7` and `/O1` are much worse.
- **Non-volatile phase bitfields** (all three spellings): 2544 B, much worse. Retail rereads the field after every store, so the field is volatile.

These diagnostic probes are not usable as source, but they locate the cause:

- Deleting the queued-bit statement makes the reset take edx, and the rest of the rotation then matches (832/863).
- Deleting the profiling CSV block, case 4, or the whole switch leaves the reset on eax.

So the choice is decided locally, by the queued-bit read-modify-write and the scoped ctor between it and the reset. WorldBuilder's body, which has asserts and /GS, shows exactly retail's registers here, which also points to a local cause.

Two diagnostic probes add throwaway volatile reads before the queued bit. With one extra read, the read takes edx, the queued bit eax and the reset ecx. With two, they take edx and eax, the queued bit ecx and the reset edx. That is a plain "previous temporary plus one" cycle (eax, ecx, edx), in which the ctor's precoloured `lea ecx` does not count.

Retail gives the queued bit and the reset the same register (edx). For that, something between them must be allocated ecx and counted. In our build nothing is.

## Next lead

The open question is what retail allocates in ecx, and counts, between the queued-bit store and the reset. That allocation must emit no instruction of its own (retail's bytes there equal ours), or it is the ctor's `this` reached through a temporary rather than a precoloured operand. Candidates not yet tried:

- a scoped type whose constructor is inline and calls the out-of-line 0x30980 body on a member at a nonzero offset;
- the queued bit and the scoped object declared in one declaration statement or one full expression;
- a check of whether the EH unwind funclet for the scoped object differs from retail's in `.text$x`. The handler is pushed at +0x02, and a different funclet would point to a different object spelling.

Do not repeat the lists above.
