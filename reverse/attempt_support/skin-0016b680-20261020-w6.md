## What moved the body

Three source changes, each measured with the unmodified `tools/align_diff.py` on a fresh trial path:

1. The two blend matrices are scaled together row by row inside one loop (`for r { a[r][0..3]*=w0; b[r][0..3]*=w1; }`), the shape of BFME 1's 0x00925860 (`m0[r3]*=w0; m1[r3]*=w1;`). The loop form matters: the same statements written out unrolled, a b-first row order, a row-scaling helper, nested inner loops and `Vector4::operator*=` on a real `Matrix3D` were all worse. This alone reproduces retail's scale region instruction for instruction (only stack displacements differed).
2. Both vertex loops use the donor's `int n=count; while(n--) {...}` instead of `if(n) do {...} while(--n)`. This removed the loop-entry spill of out-minus-second-source at esp+0x14, so the whole stack frame now matches retail (source[] at 0x14, counter at 0x58, spill at 0xA0).
3. The quaternion-to-matrix helper uses the spelling and declaration order of the matched inline helper `HTreePivotTransform::Get_Matrix3D` (0x0014D28E, `Code/Libraries/Source/WWVegas/WW3D2/fxshadernamespacesas.cpp`): `xx, xy, xz, xw, yy, yz, yw, zz, zw` read straight from `t.Rotation`. With this, matrix b's products match retail completely. Reading through a `const Quaternion &q` local instead gives 8/71/15 (see table); 24 other declaration orders of the nine products were all far worse (24 to 45 regions).

## Remaining mismatch

- Matrix a only: retail loads X, W and Y and folds Z in the `xz`, `zw` and `yz` products (0x184, 0x171, 0x210); we load Z. Matrix b matches. The same inline helper therefore gets different operand ranks in its two inlined copies in retail.
- Blend sum leaf order: retail X row is a00, a01, b00, b01, b02, a02; ours is a02, a01, b01, b02, b00, a00. Y and Z rows differ by one adjacent pair each. Source order of the sum is inert (reversed, donor order and b-first spellings compile identically); grouping into per-matrix partial sums is worse.
- Single-bone loop (all 8 structural regions): the instruction trees match retail, but the scheduler hoists y's `W*v.Y` product early, so y and z end in xmm3 and xmm1 (retail xmm1 and xmm3), and q.X is preloaded into xmm7.
