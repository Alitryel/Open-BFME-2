## Remaining mismatch (+0x572..+0x58e, case 2 relation compare)

Both compares (case 1 at +0x44c, case 2 at +0x558) are
`other->status6() || getObject()->rva0028B511()==other->rva0028B511()` in WB
(0x12316a0 and 0x1231904, byte-identical sequences). Retail compiles them differently:

    case 1 (we match):  mov ecx,[esi-8]; call; mov ecx,edi; mov ebx,eax; call; cmp ebx,eax
    case 2 retail:      mov ebx,[esi-8]; mov ecx,edi; call; mov ecx,ebx; mov [ebp-0x18],eax;
                        call; mov ecx,[ebp-0x18]; cmp eax,ecx
    case 2 ours:        same as case 1

Retail case 2 binds the getObject() value first, calls `other`'s getter first, then
the object's, and compares `eax(me), ecx(other)`. That is a tie in operand weight,
broken right-first, with the left `this` held in a temp that is not forward-substituted.

Ruled out (each n=1, case 2 only unless noted; none changed case 2 except as listed):

- operands reversed (`other->..==getObject()->..`): ours still calls getObject first;
- `obj` (function-scope local) on the left: right order, but `me` is reloaded from `[ebp-0x14]`;
- reference-returning accessor `Object *const &`: right order, but the `[esi-8]` load comes after the call;
- `Object *me=getObject();` before the `if`, either operand order: propagated, me first;
- comma `(me=getObject(), me->..==other->..)`: me first;
- two-statement accessor; base-view accessor through `this-0x10`; free helper `objectOfModule(this)`;
- `!status6 && !=` inverted branches; `bool friendly` local; `continue` form;
- `closest` in place of `other`;
- moving `closest=`/`closestDist=` after the `if` (worse: 10 regions);
- declaration order: EmotionTrackerUpdate before Object, Object after it, the getter first in Object
  (the recipe from 0x20C83F, `decl-order-call-order`);
- the inline getObject used everywhere through a reference (worse: 9 regions).

Second round (same session; the bank is now c27, which has the same score as c6):

- **Real hierarchy (c27, banked).** `EmotionModuleBase{vptr,m_data,m_object}` plus two
  interface bases, with `getObject()` in the primary base and `update()` overriding
  the third base, so the compiler supplies the `this-0x10` adjustment. Same result
  as c6 (2194 B, 2 regions, 25 memory, 3 register), but faithful, so it is the base for further work.
- **Order fixed, but frame and register allocation wrong (one shared shape, 2199 B, 3 regions, 71 memory,
  9 register).** These variants all call `other`'s getter first and load `[esi-8]` before
  it, as retail does. But MSVC keeps `data` in ebx, spills `me` to the dead `n`
  slot `[ebp-0x18]` and `other`'s result to a new slot `[ebp-0x20]`. That shifts the
  frame (`[ebp-0x24]` becomes `[ebp-0x28]`). Retail instead evicts `data` from ebx for `me` and
  spills `other`'s result to `[ebp-0x18]`. The variants:
  - c36/c39/c40: an inline wrapper on the right (`int layer() const`, also
    with a body local and with `__forceinline`), `getObject()->rva0028B511()==other->layer()`;
  - c42/c43: free inline `layerOf(o)` on both sides or on the right only;
  - c47: forceinline `sameLayer(a,b){int t=b->f(); return a->f()==t;}`;
  - c50/c51/c52: inline `Object::sameLayer(int l)` as
    `getObject()->sameLayer(other->rva0028B511())`, with plain inline,
    forceinline, and `l==f()`.
  The inlined parameters and locals become named variables, which lose ebx to `data`;
  retail's `me` behaves like an expression temp that wins ebx.
- **Same as c6:** forceinline `sameLayer(a,b){return a->f()==b->f();}` in either
  argument order (c45/c46). Inlining is transparent.
- **Worse:** `m_object` or `obj` on the left together with the right wrapper (c37/c38: 47 memory,
  10 register). Plain `inline sameLayer` (c41/c44/c48) is not inlined at /O1 here: an
  out-of-line call, 2180 B.
