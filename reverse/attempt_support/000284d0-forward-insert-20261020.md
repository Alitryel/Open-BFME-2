## Why `uninitialized_copy` is a call

Calling `__copy_trivial` directly gives the right instruction stream but a 0x6C frame instead of 0x90. The missing 0x24 bytes are the frame slots /Ob1 reserves for the inline layers of `uninitialized_copy` before it declines to expand the outer template. Mirroring STLport's chain (`uninitialized_copy` inline, then `__uninitialized_copy`, `__copy_aux` and `__copy_ptrs` as `__forceinline`, as in `reference/shims/bfmealloc`) restores every slot. The declined outer template is then called by its own name, which retail resolves to 0x000179B0.

Fold-proof for that pin: vendor STLport without the bfmealloc shim, compiled `/DNDEBUG /MD /O2 /D_STLP_USE_STATIC_LIB /D_STLP_NO_EXCEPTIONS` with `#include <memory>` and `namespace _STL { template char *uninitialized_copy<char *, char *>(char *, char *, char *); }`, emits `??$uninitialized_copy@PADPAD@_STL@@YAPADPAD00@Z` as 41 bytes equal to retail 0x000179B0, whose one relocation is `__imp__memmove`. The same TU emits `__uninitialized_copy`, `__copy_aux` and `__copy_ptrs` with the same 41 bytes, which matches the harvested `??$__copy_aux@PADPAD...` pin already at 0x000179B0. A ledger source still has to be chosen for the `fold-proof=` note.
