## What changed, and the evidence for each change

1. `capturePending` (0x9E1B28) and `captureIndex` (0x9E1CBC) are file-static. `reverse/data_xrefs.tsv` lists draw as their only reader and writer, and retail loads the takeScreenShot vptr (`mov eax,[edi]`) before `dec [capturePending]` in the final screenshot guard, which is the file-static scheduling pattern described in `docs/matching.md` (seventh pattern). With `extern` the load stays behind the store. This alone took the bank from 7 to 5 regions.
2. The do-while runs on a separate flag, as WorldBuilder 0x96E810 does (its `local_1`): `keepDrawing=false` before the loop, `keepDrawing=freezeTime&&!TheTacticalView->isCameraMovementFinished(); if(keepDrawing&&!TheGameLogic->isGamePaused())keepDrawing=false;` at the bottom, `while(keepDrawing)`. This removed the constant zero the old bank kept in EBX at the loop top and in the tail; the tail now uses ECX zero and EDX for the scripts begin pointer, as retail does. Result: 4 regions, 64 memory, 2 register.
3. Semantic fix: the field at GlobalData+0xEA8 is `TWO_PI / panoramaCount`. Retail's constant at VA 0xBC746C reads 6.2831855f (WorldBuilder: `fdivr 6.2831855f`), and the old bank wrote `1.0f`. The field is renamed `panoramaStep` in the private view; bytes are unchanged because the constant is a relocation.
4. Faithful spellings with identical bytes: function-local `static Int prevTime = timeGetTime()` and `static Int timeMultiplierCounter = 1` with `Int minTime = 30`, as in Zero Hour's draw (the hand-written guard and extern counters are gone); the converged globals `TheWritableGlobalData` (reads through a const `TheGlobalData` macro, as Zero Hour's GlobalData.h does) and `TheScriptEngine` (cast to the placeholder `Rva00203B47Host` view for the 0x00203B47 call) replace the address-named `g_pRva003BBEDE` and the second GlobalData name; the `count2` and `panCount` copies are dropped.

## Hypotheses ruled out this run

1. Writing the capture block in WorldBuilder's literal form (`new char[width*3*height*count]`, three direct count reads in the slice call, `width*count` for 0x00047BC0, no `imageArea`) does give retail's new[] product order (count, height, width, 3), but the image pointer then takes EBX and width spills, so it is not closer (1248 B, 7 to 9 regions, 9 to 11 register differences).
2. The else-arm form `add eax,0xEA0 / mov ecx,[eax]` is not produced by three direct reads, by a const or writable reference or pointer bound inside the arm or before the individual test, by a `const GlobalData*` local, or by mixing const and writable reads of the count. All of these give `mov ecx,[eax+0xEA0]` in the arm: one region fewer than the bank but one memory difference more, so by the acceptance rule they are not closer.
3. Moving the declarations of `i` and `image` (swapped, at the top of the capture block, `i` declared separately) or using a ternary for the allocation is neutral or worse.

## Remaining mismatch and resume point

Two places remain, everything else is relocation noise.

- Capture block start (retail 0x304 to 0x31C): the bank computes `imageArea = height*width` before the individual test, so it stores image as `and [ebp-0x1c],edi` early and multiplies `count*imageArea`. Retail keeps height in ECX across the test, stores image late as `mov [ebp-0x1c],edi`, and multiplies count, height, width, 3 inside the arm. The `imageArea` local exists only because it gives width enough priority to win EBX. A scratch probe that removes one image use (the delete) lets width win without it, so the gap is smaller than one use inside an `if` arm. With `i` initialised in the for statement (WorldBuilder's order) and `imageArea` kept, image is stored late from the EDI zero exactly as in retail and only the `imageArea` multiply and the height spill remain (5 regions, 64 memory, 1 register); without `imageArea` in that order, `this` takes EBX inside the loop instead (1261 B). The next experiment should look for the natural source change that raises width above both `this` and image there.
- Slice arm (retail 0x390 and 0x3DD): the bank reads the count once before the individual test; retail reads it inside the else arm through `add eax,0xEA0 / mov ecx,[eax]`.
