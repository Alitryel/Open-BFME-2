## Finding 1: the frame needs the subscript's body to be visible

With only a declaration of the rowed wrapper, the int key temporary keeps its own slot. The frame grows to 0x4C, and every local after it shifts by 4. When the callee's body is visible in the unit, as STLport's inline `operator[]` was in retail, cl lets the key temporary's slot `[ebp-0x10]` be reused for the goal-pointer spill, which gives 0x48 as in retail.

## Finding 2: retail's tail is the WorldBuilder twin's in-place scale, with its dead stores removed

The WorldBuilder twin (0x010CD770) builds the vectors this way:

1. `dir` is a copy of the object's position, then the attacker position is subtracted in place (out-of-line call).
2. `dir.normalize()`.
3. `dir.scale(10.0f * distance)` (out-of-line call).
4. `pos` is a copy of the object's position, then `pos` is added to with `&dir` (out-of-line call).
5. `goal.m_pos = pos; goal.m_value = frames`.

Both vectors sit in exception-handling states (2 and 3).

In retail, the scale's x product folds `dir.x` from memory: `movaps xmm5,xmm0; mulss xmm5,[ebp-0x30]`. `obj.y` and `obj.z` are hoisted into xmm1 and xmm2, while `obj.x` is loaded last into xmm0, the register `s` frees.

Scaling `dir` in place reproduces exactly that instruction selection and register assignment (trial `b14`). The one difference is that cl keeps the three stores of the scaled `dir`. `dir`'s address escapes to `normalize`, and the loop's next iteration calls functions that cl assumes could read it. Those stores also keep the `obj` loads below them, which accounts for the remaining +25 bytes.

Scaling a scalar copy (`offset`) avoids the stores, but then cl either loads `obj.x` early (the bank) or loads `dir.x` into a register instead of folding it (scale before `pos.set`).
